## 2. Verbatim openings and closings

### THE BIG CONVERSATION

**10 Aug — "A wellbeing program is not support."**
- Opens: *"If you're dealing with a mental health issue at work, your own GP or
  psychologist is often more valuable than any program offered by your employer."*
- Closes: *"You can't guarantee you'll get that though, so having support outside of work
  is the part you can actually count on."*

**3 Aug — "The stuff that's too small to raise and never stops."**
- Opens: *"Babe, hun, darl, buddy."* (a four-word fragment — no verb)
- Closes: *"If you're the one calling your colleagues these nicknames, keep in mind how it
  might come off because it may be too small for them to bring up with you but doesn't
  mean it's ok for you."*

**27 Jul — "Thank you for applying to this role."**
- Opens: *"Well before a hiring manager sees your name, your CV goes into an applicant
  tracking system that scans it for the words in the job ad and ranks you on how closely
  they match."*
- Closes: *"It's why a rejection for your job app isn't rigged against you and not because
  you 'aren't good enough'."* (shipped with a grammatical stumble — "isn't ... and not
  because" — left uncorrected in the live post)

**20 Jul — "Nobody is actually declining lunch."**
- Opens: *"Turning down a team lunch shouldn't be a career decision, but plenty of people
  check themselves before they do it."*
- Closes: *"Every team is different and workplace culture even more so, so it's just up to
  you to prioritise accordingly."*

**13 Jul — "Should kids be allowed in the office?"**
- Opens: *"School holidays run about twelve weeks a year."*
- Closes: *"The fallout just reappears as tanking productivity, resentful exhausted
  parents, and a fractured workplace culture."*

**6 Jul — "A shared room is not a saving."**
- Opens: *"Forcing colleagues to share a hotel room at a conference is far more common than
  most people realise."*
- Closes: *"And that only works if the rooms fill up without anyone complaining."*

**What the openings share:** five of six state the situation/mechanism directly and
plainly in sentence one, no throat-clearing, no question, no "we asked" framing — matches
the voice-guide's "name the real thing" instruction. The exception is 3 Aug, which opens
with a bare fragment (a nickname list, no verb) rather than a full sentence — a real,
recurring-enough device (see drift table) not documented anywhere in the current rules.

**What the closings share:** none are a sweeping "zoom-out" systemic finale and none carry
a sign-off — that part of the rule holds completely. But only two of six (13 Jul, 6 Jul)
land on the sharp, slightly unresolved "turn" the voice-guide asks for. The other four
close on reader-directed practical resolution ("up to you to prioritise", "you can actually
count on", "keep in mind how it might come off", reassurance to the reader) — softer,
more advice-shaped endings than the voice-guide's own gold standard describes.

## 4. Observed AI tells

### Found live in the published pieces (quoted verbatim)

- **The fake-profound reframe, shipped in a Brains Trust piece (27 Jul):** *"AI in
  Australia isn't producing mass layoffs. It's producing slower hiring..."* — structurally
  identical to the exact pattern the big-conversation voice-guide bans ("this isn't about X,
  it's about Y"). The tell list currently only governs Big Conversation drafting; this shows
  the same construction reaching print in the Brains Trust segment too, unchecked.
- **A soft "announced wrap-up" opener on the final paragraph (10 Aug Big Conversation):**
  *"In the end, your experience at work often comes down to your manager more than the
  company itself."* "In the end" signals a summary move rather than opening the paragraph
  with the new information itself — close to the "announced transitions" tell.
- **A recurring "done properly/done the right way" concession formula, used twice in six
  sampled pieces:** 13 Jul — *"Done properly, visiting your parents' place of work can be a
  good thing."* / 6 Jul — *"Done the right way, people come home with a good story."* Not on
  the banned list, but repeating almost verbatim across a 6-piece sample is a house tic
  worth watching so a generation prompt doesn't calcify it into a formula.
- **A reader-reassurance close (27 Jul):** *"It's why a rejection for your job app isn't
  rigged against you and not because you 'aren't good enough'."* Functions like the banned
  "reader-validation reach-through" — comforting the reader directly — but placed at the END
  of the piece rather than the opening the tell list describes; the tell list should note
  this can happen at either end.

### Not found (the cardinal rule holds up completely)

Scanned all six Big Conversation pieces for any of the banned phrasings ("someone wrote
in", "people said", "the replies", "many of you", "respondents said", etc.) — **zero
violations across all 6 pieces.** No screenshot, handle, or submission is ever referenced
in the prose. This is the one rule that shipped with perfect compliance in this sample.

Also not found: em dashes in editorial prose (only appear in Reddit "— Top Comment"
attribution lines, which are quoted community material, not house prose); explicit jokes
or punchlines (humour consistently lands as dry, plain-stated observation — e.g. the ironic
scare-quotes on *"that iPad kid practicing violin in a 'soundproof' meeting booth"*, 13
Jul); invented statistics without a real, named source.

### The tells the voice-guide already catalogues (for the de-AI prompt author, one list)

Fake-profound reframe; paired parallel negations; anaphora lists; snap-closer aphorism;
corporate/Latinate diction (infrastructure, leverage, robust, facilitate); "sounds
[adjective] right up until…" / "make no mistake" / "the uncomfortable truth is"；
over-signposted concessions; em-dash drama; the zoom-out ending; the trigger-vs-cause pivot;
reader-validation reach-through; the privilege-check/empathy pivot; archetype cataloguing;
announced transitions; the full-circle callback; the forced silver lining;
aphorism-after-colloquialism; teeter-totter symmetry; the safe-contrarian hook. (Full text
with examples: `.../big-conversation/references/voice-guide.md`, "AI tells to strip"
section.)

## 5. What a generation prompt must forbid / require

**Forbid, with evidence:**

- Do not claim Brains Trust is "you"-free as a hard rule without a caveat — it is broken in
  4 of the last 6 editions, including in closing sentences. A prompt that treats "zero you"
  as absolute will fight the very editors who keep letting it back in. (Drift #1)
- Do not claim pull quotes are gone. They reappeared twice in six weeks, once with a named
  economist attribution. A prompt should offer the pull-quote format as a legitimate
  optional device again, not suppress it entirely. (Drift #2)
- Do not overstate the Brains Trust word ceiling at 350 — no recent piece gets within 90
  words of it. Calibrate the working target closer to 225-260. (Drift #4)
- Do not overstate the Big Conversation paragraph range as "4-6" — write for 4 as the
  default, with 5 as an occasional, not routine, stretch. 6 has not shipped in this sample.
  (Drift #7)
- Do not let Big Conversation closings default to "up to you" / "it depends" / direct
  reassurance. That is a real, observed softening pattern in half the recent sample and it
  works against the voice-guide's own "end on a turn, don't resolve into a tidy lesson"
  instruction. (Drift #9, tell section)
- Do not let the fake-profound "isn't X, it's Y" reframe pattern into Brains Trust drafts —
  it already got through once. The tell list needs to apply beyond Big Conversation.

**Require, with evidence:**

- Require Brains Trust openings to state a number or event in sentence one — 4 of 6 do this
  well (10 Aug, 20 Jul, 13 Jul, 6 Jul) and it reads strongest; the 2 that open on a
  conceptual/paradox frame (3 Aug, 27 Jul) read comparatively softer.
- Require Big Conversation P1 to name the mechanism plainly and directly, no throat-clearing
  — 5 of 6 pieces do exactly this and it is the strongest, most consistent shipped pattern
  in the whole sample.
- Require the cardinal "never reference the submissions" rule to stay exactly as strict as
  it is — it is the one rule with 6/6 perfect compliance in this sample and should not be
  loosened.
- Require closing paragraphs (both segments) to be checked for a genuine turn/mechanism
  rather than a stat pile or reassurance — only 2 of 6 Brains Trust and 2 of 6 Big
  Conversation closings currently nail this, so it needs active enforcement, not passive
  hope.
- Where Brains Trust names real employers/companies as positive contrast examples (13 Jul:
  IAG, SEEK, Dexus, with real dollar figures), that specificity reads well and should be
  kept as an option, not just industry-category language ("banks, insurers, telcos").
